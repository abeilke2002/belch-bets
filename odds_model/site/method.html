Methodology

How a pick is made

Every game day at noon ET, a model reads the official injury report and the player-points lines at four sportsbooks, then bets every prop where it finds enough of an edge. This page explains each step, the rule it follows, and how it has done.

The short version

Sportsbooks are good at setting lines, so the model doesn't try to out-guess them from scratch. It starts from what the books' prices say, then adjusts a little when its own points projection disagrees with the line. When that adjusted probability beats the best available price by at least 2 percentage points, and the player is in a clean spot, it bets. It bets every spot that clears that bar, usually a few a night, at the same stake each.

01 The points projection

The first model predicts how many points each player will score tonight, using only information available before tip-off. It works in two stages, because points depend so heavily on playing time:

Minutes

Predicts tonight's minutes from the player's season and recent minutes, how often he starts, rest, games played, whether he's just back from an absence, and how many shots his missing teammates usually take.

Points

Uses the predicted minutes plus season and recent scoring, shot and free-throw volume, last season's average, starter role, rest, the opponent's defense against his position, and who is out.

Injuries matter most. At noon the model reads the NBA's official injury report. Players listed Out or Doubtful count as missing; Questionable players count as half missing, because they end up playing about half the time. When a teammate is out, the shots he usually takes are shared among the remaining players in proportion to how much each one normally shoots. The model is built so that a teammate sitting can only raise, never lower, another player's projection.

Uncertainty. Every projection comes with a typical miss (the "±" on the site), measured from how far past projections of the same size landed from the real result. A 25-point projection is less certain than a 6-point one, in points, and the ± reflects that.

Both stages are gradient-boosted tree models (LightGBM) trained on every regular-season game since 2018-19, and every input is checked to use only games before the one being predicted.

02 From projection to probability

A projection alone isn't a bet. A projection of 11.5 against a line of 14.5 sounds like a strong under, but the books may know something the box scores don't. So the model starts from the market.

  • The market's view. For each prop, take the over and under prices at each of the four books, remove the books' built-in margin (the "vig"), and use the middle value. That's the market's probability of the over.
  • The nudge. Move that probability toward the projection's side, by an amount that depends on how far the projection is from the line, measured in units of its uncertainty.
// log-odds of the over z = ln( P_market / (1 − P_market) ) // the market's view, kept at full weight − 0.036 // small constant − 0.018 × (projection − line) + 0.347 × (projection − line) / σ // the projection's pull, scaled by its uncertainty P_over = 1 / (1 + e^−z) P_under = 1 − P_over

The weights are learned from three seasons of real lines and results, with the most recent season counted most (each older season counts half as much as the next). Keeping the market at full weight was a deliberate choice: in testing, letting the model drift further from the market made it overconfident. With this design, the picks it rated at 56% won 56% of the time.

03 Edge: probability vs price

Every price implies a break-even win rate. At −110 you need to win 52.4% just to break even; at +100, 50%. The edge is the model's probability minus the break-even of the best price on that side across DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM and Caesars.

break-even at −110 = 110 / (110 + 100) = 52.4% break-even at +105 = 100 / (105 + 100) = 48.8% edge = P(side) − break-even(best price)
Example

Kawhi Leonard under 24.5, Oct 28, 2025. The model gives the under 55.9%. BetMGM had the best under price at −115 (break-even 53.5%), so the edge is +2.4%. DraftKings at −119 would have been only +1.5%, which is why shopping the four books matters. He scored 18.

04 The daily bets

Out of every prop on the slate, the rule bets every one that passes all three:

Skip injury-report players

Anyone listed Probable, Questionable or Doubtful at noon. Their minutes are a coin flip and the lines move late.

Skip back-to-backs

Second night of back-to-back games: rest decisions and minute limits make these the least predictable props.

Edge of 2% or more

Every remaining prop that clears 2% is a bet. In testing that was about three a night on average, more on busy nights, and some nights none.

And no bets at all from April 1 to the end of the regular season, about its last two weeks. Teams rest starters, tank and try out young players, so bench players' lines jump with their new minutes. The books see it coming; the model only knows each player's season-long role and bets the under. In testing those April unders lost heavily in two of three seasons, and the line moved against them after noon in all three.

And no bets on a team for its first three games after it loses a player midseason (a trade, or a release he signs elsewhere from). The players left behind pick up his minutes and shots, the books adjust their lines first, and the model is still working from the old roles. In testing those bets won only 42%, in both seasons, mostly around the trade deadline. The traded player himself is fine to bet once he has played for his new team; until then the site skips him, because it can't project a role it hasn't seen.

Why all of them and not just the best one? The whole model was retrained 10 different ways and replayed on three seasons. Betting every 2%+ edge made money in all 10 runs; taking only the day's top edge swung from a loss to a profit depending on the run. More bets spread the luck out.

05 Stake size

Every bet is 1 unit. The model overstates small edges: bets it rated 2% to 4% beat break-even by only about 1% in testing, so sizing up on its confidence isn't justified. With several bets a night, make your unit smaller than you would for a single pick. A quarter to a half of a normal bet is a sensible start.

Over 2024-26 that's about +6.3% per bet, against a break-even win rate of about 52%. The statistical range runs from roughly 0% to +12%. Because two of the rules came from these same seasons, expect the live result to land lower; treat it as promising, not proven. Expect cold spells: the worst losing stretch averaged about 13 units.

The strongest evidence isn't the profit. Between noon and tip-off, the market moved toward the model's side about 60% of the time and away from it 40%, in every season and every retraining. That is very unlikely to be luck, and it means the projections spot prices the market later corrects. Whether that edge is big enough to beat the books' margin is what this season will show. One caution: the model running live was refit on all three seasons and leans on the projection about half as much as these backtest models did, so expect fewer bets than the backtest shows.

What the model can't see

  • News after noon. Late scratches, starting-lineup announcements and minute limits arrive after the pick is made.
  • Role changes the box score hasn't shown yet. When a bench player's line jumps because a star is out, the books often know he's moving into the rotation; the model only knows his history. These "fade the jump" bets have been profitable, but the model's confidence in them runs too high.
  • The first weeks of a season, when averages rest on a handful of games.
  • The end of the regular season, when rotations change for reasons the box score can't show. That's why the site sits out April.
  • Anything a sportsbook knows that public data doesn't. That's why the model leans on the market instead of replacing it.

Glossary

Line
The points total the book sets, e.g. 14.5. Over wins at 15+, under at 14 or fewer.
Price (odds)
American odds. −110 means risk 110 to win 100; +105 means risk 100 to win 105.
Break-even
The win rate a price needs to make money over time.
No-vig probability
The books' implied probability with their margin removed, so over and under add to 100%.
Projection ± σ
The model's expected points, and its typical miss for a projection that size.
Market P / Model P
The books' probability of the over, and the model's probability of the side it picks.
Edge
Model probability minus the break-even of the best price. 2% is the minimum to bet.
Unit (u)
Your standard bet size. Every bet is 1 unit.
Closing line / CLV
The line right before tip-off. If it moved toward our side (e.g. we took under 14.5 and it closed 13.5), the market came around to our view: a good sign even when a single bet loses.
Back-to-back
A team's second game on consecutive nights.
